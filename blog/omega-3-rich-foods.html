5 Omega-3 Rich Foods to Try

S

By Savv
November 2026 · 4 min read

Omega-3s are a family of fats your body uses but cannot make on its own, so they have to come from food. Capsules get most of the attention, but the food sources are genuinely tasty. Here are five of the best-known ones, with honest notes on how to actually eat each.

Two kinds of omega-3, five easy foods

A quick bit of label literacy: fatty fish provide omega-3s called EPA and DHA directly, while plant foods like walnuts and seeds provide a related one called ALA, which the body converts only in small amounts. Both kinds count as part of a varied diet. The simplest approach is to rotate a few of these foods through your week instead of chasing a perfect daily number.

  1. Salmon. The classic. A salmon fillet is one of the richest everyday sources of EPA and DHA, and it is forgiving to cook: bake it at a moderate heat until it flakes, or pan-fry it skin-side down for a crisp finish. Fresh, frozen, and canned salmon all count, so use whichever fits the budget and the week.
  2. Sardines. The underrated one. These small fish pack omega-3s comparable to salmon, usually at a fraction of the price, and they come ready to eat straight from the tin. Mash them on toast with lemon and black pepper, or stir them through pasta. If the idea is new to you, start with sardines packed in olive oil; the flavour is milder than their reputation.
  3. Walnuts. The standout nut for ALA, the plant omega-3. A small handful works as a snack, but walnuts also disappear happily into oatmeal, salads, and baking. Store them in the fridge or freezer if you buy in bulk; like all nuts, they keep their flavour longer away from heat and light.
  4. Ground flaxseed. Flax is one of the richest plant sources of ALA, and the ground part matters: whole flaxseeds can pass through mostly undigested, while ground flax mixes invisibly into smoothies, oatmeal, yogurt, and pancake batter. A tablespoon a day is an easy habit to keep. Buy it ground or grind small batches yourself and keep it refrigerated.
  5. Chia seeds. Another ALA source, famous for absorbing liquid and turning into a gel. That trick makes chia pudding (chia, milk, a little honey, overnight in the fridge) and also makes the seeds an easy thickener for smoothies. Dry, they sprinkle over salads and toast without changing the flavour of anything.
