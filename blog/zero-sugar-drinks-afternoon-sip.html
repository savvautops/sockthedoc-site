5 Zero Sugar Drinks for a Lighter Afternoon Sip

S

By Savv
October 2026 · 5 min read

The afternoon drink break is a ritual worth keeping. The sugary soda or sweet coffee that usually fills it is optional. These five sips keep the cold glass, the fizz, and the pause, with no sugar in the glass and nothing you need a specialty store to find.

A note from Sock: Sock is a duck, not a doctor. This post is about drinks to enjoy, shared for information and pleasure only, not medical advice, and not a treatment or weight loss plan. If you manage a health condition that involves what you drink, check with a qualified health professional about your own situation.

1. Sparkling water with citrus

The simplest swap on this list, and the one that sticks. Plain sparkling water does the fizzy work; fresh citrus does the flavour work.

  • Fill a tall glass with ice and pour in plain sparkling water.
  • Squeeze in half a lemon or lime, then drop the squeezed half into the glass.
  • Want it fancier: add a thin slice of orange or a few strips of lemon peel and let it sit for two minutes.

2. Unsweetened iced tea with mint

Iced tea without the sugar is a different drink entirely: crisp, a little bitter in a good way, and very cold. Mint makes it taste like you tried harder than you did.

  • Brew a strong pot of black or green tea, 4 tea bags in about 4 cups of hot water, and let it cool. Fridge cold is best.
  • Pour over ice with a small handful of fresh mint leaves, lightly pressed with the back of a spoon to wake them up.
  • Add a lemon slice if you like it bright. No sweetener needed once it is properly cold.

3. Fizzy berry water

Muddled berries give sparkling water real colour and flavour, and the glass looks like a drink you would pay café money for.

  • Put a small handful of raspberries, strawberries, or blueberries (fresh or thawed from frozen) in the bottom of a tall glass.
  • Press them gently with a spoon until they give up their juice, then fill the glass with ice.
  • Top with sparkling water, stir once, and sip before the berries settle. Eating the berries at the end is part of the deal.

4. Cucumber mint infused water

The spa water, minus the spa. Make a pitcher in the morning and the afternoon pour is already waiting for you.

  • Slice half a cucumber thinly and add it to a pitcher of cold water with a small handful of mint leaves.
  • Rest it in the fridge for at least 1 hour; overnight is even better.
  • Pour over ice and refill the pitcher once before starting fresh the next day.

5. Iced hibiscus tea

Dried hibiscus brews into a deep red, tart, cranberry-like tea that tastes like it must have sugar in it. It does not. This is the afternoon sip for people who find plain water boring and are not sorry about it.

  • Steep 2 tablespoons of dried hibiscus flowers in 4 cups of hot water for about 10 minutes, then strain.
  • Let it cool, then pour over plenty of ice.
  • Finish with a squeeze of lime or an orange slice. If it is too tart at first, brew it a little weaker rather than reaching for a sweetener.
