5 Easy Amaranth Breakfasts to Try

S

By Savv
November 2026 · 4 min read

Amaranth is a tiny seed-like grain that has been eaten for thousands of years and is quietly showing up in pantries again. It cooks like a small, nutty porridge grain, it is naturally gluten free, and it plays well with everything already in your breakfast rotation. Here are five easy ways in.

One grain, five breakfasts

The basic cook is simple: simmer 1 part amaranth in about 2.5 parts water or milk for 20 to 25 minutes until the grains turn translucent and the pot looks like porridge. Everything below starts from that pot, or from popped or flaked versions of the same grain. Start with whichever breakfast already sounds like your kind of morning.

  1. Warm amaranth porridge with berries. The starter recipe. Simmer amaranth in milk or water until it thickens like oatmeal, then top with a handful of berries and a drizzle of honey or maple syrup. The grains stay slightly chewy, which keeps it interesting next to the soft fruit. Leftovers reheat fine with a splash of milk.
  2. Amaranth overnight oats. Stir cooked amaranth together with rolled oats, milk or yogurt, and a spoon of chia seeds, then park it in the fridge overnight. By morning it is thick, cold, and ready to eat straight from the jar. Add fruit in the morning so it stays fresh, and breakfast is officially a grab-and-go.
  3. Amaranth pancakes. Swap part of your usual pancake flour for amaranth flour, or fold a cup of cooked amaranth into your regular batter. The pancakes come out a little denser and nuttier, in a good way. They freeze well, so a Sunday batch can cover weekday mornings too.
  4. An amaranth smoothie bowl. Blend frozen fruit, banana, and yogurt into a thick smoothie, pour it into a bowl, and scatter popped amaranth over the top for crunch. Popped amaranth is sold ready-made and puffs like miniature popcorn. The contrast of cold smoothie and crunchy topping is the whole point.
  5. Amaranth with yogurt and honey. The five-minute option. Spoon warm or leftover cooked amaranth over Greek yogurt, add a drizzle of honey and a pinch of cinnamon. It eats like a parfait, takes almost no effort, and works just as well as an afternoon snack as it does at breakfast.
