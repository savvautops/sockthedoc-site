5 Vietnamese Coffee Drinks for a Slow Morning Sip

S

By Savv
October 2026 · 5 min read

Vietnamese coffee is not a grab-and-go drink. It drips slowly, it is unapologetically sweet, and it turns an ordinary morning into a small ceremony. Here are five classics you can make in a regular kitchen, no fancy gear required.

A note from Sock: Sock is a duck, not a doctor. This post is about drinks to enjoy, shared for information and pleasure only, not health advice. One honest heads-up: Vietnamese coffee is usually made with robusta beans, which pack more caffeine than the arabica in most regular coffee. Sip accordingly, especially if caffeine and you have a complicated relationship.

1. Iced coffee with condensed milk (ca phe sua da)

The famous one. Strong coffee brewed over sweetened condensed milk, stirred, then poured over a tall glass of ice. It starts out bold and dark, then melts into something that tastes like coffee candy.

  • Add 2 to 3 tablespoons of sweetened condensed milk to a heatproof glass.
  • Brew strong coffee on top (a phin filter is traditional, see drink 2; a small French press works too) and stir until the milk dissolves.
  • Fill a tall glass with ice, pour the coffee over, and stir again before sipping.

2. Hot black coffee from a phin filter (ca phe den)

A phin is a small metal filter that sits right on your cup and drips coffee one patient drop at a time. No paper filters, no machine, no rushing it. The result is a small, intense cup you drink slowly.

  • Rinse the phin with hot water and set it on top of your cup.
  • Add about 4 teaspoons of medium-coarse ground coffee, shake level, and rest the press insert on top.
  • Pour a splash of hot water (just off the boil) and wait 30 seconds for the coffee to bloom, then fill the phin, cover with the lid, and let it drip, about 4 to 5 minutes.
  • Drink it black, or add a teaspoon of sugar if the robusta bite is a bit much on day one.

3. Coconut coffee (ca phe cot dua)

Coffee meets coconut in a frosty, almost dessert-like sip that coffee shops in Vietnam serve blended thick with ice. It is rich, so a small glass is plenty.

  • Brew a strong half cup of coffee and let it cool a little.
  • Blend it with a handful of ice, 2 to 3 tablespoons of coconut milk, and condensed milk to taste until it turns slushy.
  • Pour into a small glass and eat the first few spoonfuls like a coffee snow cone. That is allowed.

4. Egg coffee (ca phe trung)

Hanoi's most surprising cup. A whipped egg yolk and condensed milk foam floats on top of strong black coffee, like a tiny tiramisu you drink. It sounds odd and tastes like custard.

  • Brew a small, strong cup of black coffee and keep it hot.
  • Whisk 1 egg yolk with 1 to 2 tablespoons of condensed milk until pale and fluffy, about 2 minutes of honest whisking.
  • Spoon the foam over the coffee. Sip the coffee up through the foam, then stir the last bit together.

5. Bac xiu

Think of bac xiu as ca phe sua da's gentler sibling: lots of milk, a modest splash of coffee, served over ice. It was made for people who love the ritual but want less coffee in the cup.

  • Fill a tall glass with ice and pour in cold milk (dairy or your favourite alternative) until the glass is about two thirds full.
  • Add 1 to 2 tablespoons of condensed milk and stir.
  • Float a shot or two of strong brewed coffee on top, stir as much or as little as you like, and sip slowly.
