5 Side Sleeper Tips for Comfier Nights

S

By Savv
November 2026 · 4 min read

Most adults sleep on their side, and most side sleepers have never once thought about their setup. A pillow in the wrong place here, a too-flat pillow there, and you wake up with a cranky shoulder wondering why. These five tweaks fix the geometry, not your whole life.

A note from Sock: Sock is a duck, not a doctor. These are general sleep-comfort ideas for informational purposes only, not medical advice. If you snore loudly, gasp in your sleep, or have ongoing pain that keeps you up, talk to a qualified health professional; those can be signs of conditions worth checking out.

Why the setup matters more than the position

Side sleeping itself is a perfectly good position. The comfort problems usually come from gaps: the space between your head and the mattress, the space between your knees, the unsupported arm with nowhere to go. Fill the gaps and the position mostly takes care of itself. Here are the five fixes, in the order most people feel them.

  1. Hug a pillow to your chest. Your top arm needs a job. Without one, it drags your shoulder forward and twists your upper back all night. Hugging a pillow (a regular bed pillow or a body pillow) keeps your shoulders stacked and your chest open. It also stops the classic dead-arm-under-the-pillow move.
  2. Keep a pillow between your knees. When your top leg slides forward and down, your hips rotate and your lower back follows. A pillow between your knees keeps your hips stacked and your spine in a straighter line. A thinner pillow works fine; you are filling a gap, not building a wall.
  3. Choose a pillow that fills the shoulder gap. Side sleepers need a taller, firmer pillow than back sleepers, because your shoulder creates a bigger space between your head and the mattress. The goal is a straight line from your head through your spine. If your head tips down toward the bed or props up at an angle, the pillow height is wrong.
  4. Sleep with a slightly raised head. A small lift, from an extra inch of pillow height or a slight incline, is a popular comfort tweak for side sleepers who wake up stuffy or with a heavy head. Keep it slight: a steep angle bends your neck and trades one ache for another.
  5. Stretch your hips before bed. Two minutes is plenty. Gentle hip flexor stretches or a figure-four stretch on each side loosens the muscles that spend all day shortened in a chair. Looser hips settle into the side position more easily and fidget less through the night.
