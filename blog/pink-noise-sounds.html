5 Pink Noise Sounds to Try Tonight

S

By Savv
November 2026 · 4 min read

White noise is the famous one, but a lot of people find it hissy and harsh. Pink noise is its softer relative: the energy is balanced toward the lower frequencies, so it sounds deeper and more like steady rain than like a detuned radio. Here are five pink-noise-style sounds to sample tonight.

A note from Sock: Sock is a duck, not a doctor. These are general sleep-comfort ideas for informational purposes only, not medical advice, and background sound is not a treatment for any sleep condition. If you snore loudly, gasp in your sleep, or struggle with persistent insomnia, talk to a qualified health professional.

How to run the experiment

Keep it simple: pick one sound per night, play it at a low, steady volume (you should be able to hear a whisper over it), and give it the whole night rather than judging it in the first five minutes. Steady sounds help mostly by smoothing out the bumps, the door slams and hallway noises that would otherwise poke holes in your sleep. After a few nights you will know which texture your brain prefers.

  1. Soft rain on leaves. The textbook pink noise sound. Rain on foliage has that balanced, rustling wash with no sharp edges, which is exactly why it anchors most pink noise playlists. It is a great first try if you are coming from white noise and found it too bright.
  2. A low fan hum. The original sleep sound, decades before apps existed. A real fan or a good fan recording gives you a deep, even hum with zero variation to listen to. Bonus if you use an actual fan: moving air and a cooler room are sleep-friendly on their own.
  3. Gentle ocean waves. Waves roll in and out rather than staying perfectly flat, and some people prefer that slow rhythm to a constant tone. Look for recordings labelled gentle or distant surf rather than dramatic crashing waves, which can be more stimulating than soothing.
  4. A quiet forest at night. Soft wind through trees, the occasional distant bird settling in, nothing sudden. Forest recordings lean natural and layered, which suits people who find pure machine tones a bit sterile. Check the preview first; some forest tracks sneak in crickets loud enough to become the main event.
  5. Steady rainfall. Plain rain, no leaves, no thunder: the sound of rain on a roof or a window, even and unchanging. This is the most metronome-like option on the list and a favourite for light sleepers in noisy buildings, because it masks irregular sounds like traffic and neighbours without drawing attention to itself.
