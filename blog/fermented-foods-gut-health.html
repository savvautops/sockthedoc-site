Fermented Foods for a Happy Gut: 7 Cabbage Plates & 6 Pickle Jars

S

By Savv
November 2026 · 5 min read

Cabbage is having a genuine moment: Pinterest Predicts named it the number one food trend for 2026, and searches for fermented cabbage are up about 35%. It is easy to see why. Cabbage is cheap, crunchy, and endlessly adaptable, and tangy fermented vegetables are one of the oldest ways to add flavour and variety to everyday meals. Here are seven cozy cabbage plates and six easy pickle jars to make at home.

7 cozy cabbage plates for happy gut meals

Cabbage is a fibre-rich vegetable that shows up in comfort food all over the world, often fermented or pickled, sometimes simply cooked until sweet and tender. These seven plates rotate nicely through a week without repeating the same flavour twice.

  1. Cabbage dumplings. Finely chopped cabbage folded into a simple filling makes dumplings that are juicy, savoury, and very forgiving for first-time wrappers. Steam them, pan-fry them, or do both for crisp bottoms and soft tops.
  2. Cozy cabbage soup. The budget hero of the list. Cabbage, onion, carrot, and tomato simmered in broth tastes like it took all afternoon and costs very little. It also freezes well, so make the big pot.
  3. Stir fried cabbage. Shredded cabbage in a hot pan with garlic and a splash of soy sauce, cooked just until the edges char and the middle stays crisp. Ten minutes, one pan, and it goes with almost anything.
  4. Fresh cabbage slaw. Thin-sliced cabbage with a bright vinegar dressing instead of a heavy mayo one. Crunchy, tangy, and better after an hour in the fridge, when the cabbage softens slightly and drinks up the dressing.
  5. Roasted cabbage wedges. Thick wedges roasted at a high heat until the edges go dark and sweet. A drizzle of oil, salt, and patience is genuinely all it takes; the oven does the work.
  6. Stuffed cabbage rolls. Tender cabbage leaves wrapped around a savoury filling and baked in tomato sauce. This is the weekend project of the list, and the leftovers are famously better on day two.
  7. Sauteed bok choy. Cabbage's leafy cousin, quickly sauteed with garlic and a little sesame oil. It wilts in minutes, keeps its bright green colour, and rounds out the week with something lighter.

6 easy artisan pickle jars to make at home

Pickling is the easiest entry point into fermented-style flavours at home. A basic brine of vinegar, water, and salt, poured over prepared vegetables and left to sit in the fridge, gives you crisp, tangy jars within a day or two. Start with whichever vegetable you already buy.

  1. Pickled cucumbers. The classic jar. Slice them into coins or spears, add garlic and dill if you like, and they are snack-ready almost overnight. The gateway pickle.
  2. Pickled carrots. Cut into sticks, carrots stay properly crunchy in brine and turn a gentle sweet-sour. Good on sandwiches, grain bowls, and straight from the jar.
  3. Pickled radish. Radishes lose their sharp bite in brine and keep their crunch, turning a pretty pink at the edges. A small jar brightens tacos, noodles, and rice bowls.
  4. Pickled red onions. Possibly the highest-impact jar on this list. Thin-sliced red onions go vivid pink and sweet-tart in about an hour, and they improve nearly every savoury plate they touch.
  5. Pickled beets. Earthy, sweet, and a deep ruby colour that stains everything it meets, in the best way. Slice them thin or cube them, and save the brine for salad dressings.
  6. Pickled green beans with dill. Crisp beans, a sprig of dill, and a garlic clove or two. They keep their snap for weeks in the fridge and make an excellent crunchy side or garnish.
