Movement

A Gentle 10-Minute Morning Routine for Stiff Backs and Sore Joints

S

By Savv
September 2026 · 6 min read

You know the feeling: the alarm goes off, you sit up, and everything from your neck to your knees files a formal complaint. Here’s a gentle, low-impact way to negotiate with your body before the day begins.

A note from Sock: Sock is a duck, not a doctor. This article shares general movement ideas for informational purposes only, not medical advice. If you have an injury, chronic pain, arthritis, or any medical condition, check with a qualified health professional before starting a new routine. Stop any movement that causes sharp or worsening pain.

Why mornings feel the worst

Overnight, your body barely moves for hours. Joints get a little less lubricated, muscles cool down and tighten, and spinal discs rehydrate and become temporarily stiffer. That’s why the first 30 minutes of the day often feel creakier than the rest of it. That’s normal physiology, not a personal failing.

The good news: gentle movement is one of the simplest ways to tell your body “we’re awake now.” You don’t need a workout. You need a warm-up for being a person.

The 10-minute routine

Do these in order, moving slowly and breathing normally throughout. If anything hurts sharply, skip it. Discomfort that eases as you move is fine, pain that gets worse is a stop sign.

Minutes 0–2: Wake up in bed

Yes, really. Start before your feet touch the floor.

  • Knee hugs (30 seconds each side): Lying on your back, pull one knee gently toward your chest and hold. You should feel a mild stretch through the lower back and glute. Switch sides.
  • Gentle spinal twist (30 seconds each side): Still on your back, drop both bent knees slowly to one side while keeping your shoulders on the mattress. Hold, breathe, switch.

Minutes 2–5: Standing loosening

  • Neck rolls (1 minute): Slowly roll your head in a wide circle, 30 seconds each direction. Keep shoulders dropped away from your ears.
  • Shoulder shrugs and rolls (1 minute): Shrug shoulders up to your ears, hold two seconds, drop. Then roll them forward and backward in big slow circles.
  • Hip circles (1 minute): Hands on hips, draw big slow circles with your hips like a hula hoop, 30 seconds each direction. This one feels silly and works wonders.

Minutes 5–8: The big three stretches

  • Standing cat-cow (1 minute): Hands on knees, slightly bent. Round your back like an angry cat, then arch it gently like a cow. Flow between the two slowly. It mobilizes the whole spine.
  • Doorway chest opener (1 minute): Stand in a doorway, forearms on the frame, and lean gently forward until you feel a stretch across the chest and front of the shoulders. Counteracts a night of curled-up sleeping.
  • Standing figure-four (1 minute, 30 seconds per side): Hold a wall or chair for balance. Cross one ankle over the opposite knee and sit back slightly, as if into an invisible chair. You’ll feel it in the outer hip and glute.

Minutes 8–10: Walk it out

Finish with two minutes of easy walking: around the house, to the kitchen, wherever. Swing your arms naturally. This tells your nervous system the day has officially started and gets blood moving to all those freshly loosened joints.

Make it stick (the duck’s real advice)

The routine isn’t the hard part. Remembering to do it is. Three tricks that actually work:

  1. Anchor it. Do it right after something you never skip, like brushing your teeth. “After teeth, I stretch” beats willpower every time.
  2. Lower the bar. On terrible mornings, just do the in-bed portion. Two minutes still counts, and it keeps the streak alive.
  3. Keep the space ready. If you need a chair for balance, leave it in the same spot. Removing friction is more powerful than adding motivation.

When to see a real professional

Gentle movement helps everyday stiffness, but it’s not a treatment. See a doctor or physiotherapist if you notice numbness or tingling, pain that shoots down a leg or arm, stiffness that doesn’t ease after moving around, or any pain that’s getting worse over weeks. Ducks support early checkups.
