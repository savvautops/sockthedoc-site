5 High Protein Dinners to Try

S

By Savv
November 2026 · 4 min read

A dinner with a solid serving of protein tends to keep you full through the evening, which is exactly when the snack cupboard starts calling. No powders or specialty products here: these five dinners build protein out of ordinary whole foods you can find in any grocery store.

A note from Sock: Sock is a duck, not a doctor. These are general meal ideas for informational purposes only, not medical or nutrition advice, and protein needs vary a lot from person to person. If you have a medical condition or specific dietary needs, talk to a qualified health professional before changing how you eat.

The pattern behind all five

Each dinner pairs a main protein with a second, quieter protein source: chicken with quinoa, salmon with lentils, turkey with beans. Stacking two moderate sources gets you to a filling plate without supersizing any single ingredient, and it keeps the meals varied enough to rotate all week. Pick the two that sound best and start there.

  1. A chicken breast bowl with quinoa. Season and cook a chicken breast, slice it over fluffy quinoa, and add whatever vegetables are in the crisper. Quinoa brings its own protein to the bowl, so the plate adds up fast. A squeeze of lemon and a spoon of hot sauce or tzatziki keeps it from tasting like a gym poster.
  2. A salmon plate with lentils. A baked or pan-fried salmon fillet next to a scoop of seasoned lentils is one of the easiest high protein plates there is. Lentils cook in about 20 minutes with no soaking, and they soak up garlic, cumin, or a splash of vinegar beautifully. Add something green and dinner is done.
  3. A turkey chili with beans. Ground turkey, canned beans, tomatoes, onion, and chili spices, simmered in one pot. Chili is batch-cooking royalty: it tastes better the next day, freezes well, and the beans quietly add protein and fibre alongside the turkey. Serve it over rice or eat it straight with a spoon, standing at the counter, as tradition demands.
  4. A tofu stir fry with edamame. The plant-based heavyweight. Press and cube firm tofu, crisp it in a hot pan, then toss with vegetables, shelled edamame, and a simple soy-ginger sauce. Tofu and edamame are both soy-based proteins, so together they make this a genuinely filling meatless dinner, not a side dish in disguise.
  5. A Greek yogurt chicken salad. Shred leftover or rotisserie chicken and dress it with Greek yogurt instead of all mayonnaise, plus celery, mustard, and herbs. The yogurt keeps it creamy while adding protein of its own. Serve it in lettuce cups, on whole grain bread, or over a simple green salad for the lightest dinner on this list.
