5 Blue Light Habits for Easier Evenings

S

By Savv
November 2026 · 4 min read

Your body reads light as information. Bright, blue-rich light says daytime; dim, warm light says the day is ending. Most of us spend the evening under the lighting equivalent of high noon, then wonder why sleep feels far away. These five habits turn the dial down gradually, no cave required.

A note from Sock: Sock is a duck, not a doctor. These are general evening-habit ideas for informational purposes only, not medical advice. Light habits are a comfort tweak, not a treatment for any condition. If you struggle with persistent insomnia or another sleep problem, talk to a qualified health professional.

Think dimmer switch, not off switch

You do not need to give up screens after dinner or live by candlelight. The realistic goal is a ramp: light gets lower and warmer as bedtime gets closer, so your body gets the hint over an hour or two instead of all at once. Each habit below shaves a little brightness off the evening. Stack two or three and the ramp builds itself.

  1. Dim the screens after dinner. The simplest habit on the list. Turn phone, tablet, and TV brightness down to match the room instead of running them at daylight levels. Most screens are far brighter than they need to be indoors at night. If your screen is the brightest thing in the room, it is winning an argument with your bedtime.
  2. Switch on night mode. Every major phone and computer has a night or warm-tone mode that shifts the display toward amber in the evening. Set it to turn on automatically at sunset or a fixed hour, then forget about it. It will not transform your sleep on its own, but it removes one blue-rich light source without any nightly effort from you.
  3. Wear blue light glasses. Glasses with amber or orange-tinted lenses filter out blue wavelengths before they reach your eyes, which is handy for the hours when you are still on a laptop or watching TV. They are a reasonable extra layer for screen-heavy evenings. Look for lenses actually tinted for evening use rather than nearly clear fashion pairs, and treat them as one habit among five, not a magic shield.
  4. Swap the bedside lamp for a warm bulb. A warm-white bulb (look for 2700K or lower on the box) in the lamp you read by changes the whole feel of the last hour. Cool daylight bulbs in the bedroom are working against you at exactly the wrong time. This is a one-time, few-dollar swap that pays off every single night.
  5. Charge the phone outside the bedroom. The nuclear option, and the most effective one here. A phone charging in the hallway cannot be scrolled in bed, cannot light up with a 1 a.m. notification, and cannot turn "one quick check" into forty minutes. If you use it as an alarm, a cheap alarm clock costs less than a week of bad sleep.
