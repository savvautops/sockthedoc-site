5 Eye Mask Picks for Darker Nights

S

By Savv
November 2026 · 4 min read

Blackout curtains are lovely, but they do not help with a streetlight through a rental window, a 5 a.m. summer sunrise, or a partner reading beside you. An eye mask is the portable version of a dark room. The trick is picking the style your face will actually tolerate all night, and they are not all the same.

A note from Sock: Sock is a duck, not a doctor. These are general sleep-comfort ideas for informational purposes only, not medical advice, and a mask is a light-blocking tool, not a treatment for any sleep condition. If you struggle with persistent insomnia or another sleep problem, talk to a qualified health professional.

Match the mask to the annoyance

Start by naming your actual problem. Light leaking around a flat mask calls for a contoured or better-sealing style. Puffy, tired-feeling eyes call for cooling. A mask that slides off by 3 a.m. calls for a better strap. Here are the five main styles, and who each one suits.

  1. A contoured silk mask. The crowd favourite. Contoured masks are moulded into cups, so the fabric floats over your eyelids instead of pressing on them; you can blink freely and your lashes never touch cloth. Silk feels cool and smooth against skin and is gentle around the eyes. If you have never worn a mask because flat ones annoyed you, start here.
  2. A cooling gel mask. Keep it in the fridge and put it on as you get into bed. The cool weight feels wonderful on tired, puffy-feeling eyes after a long screen day, and the darkness does the rest. Gel masks warm up over 20 minutes or so, which is fine: by then you are ideally asleep, and many people take them off half-asleep without noticing.
  3. A blackout mask with an adjustable strap. The maximum-darkness option. These are cut wider, often with a nose bridge shape that seals the gap where light usually sneaks in, and the adjustable strap lets you set the fit instead of accepting whatever the elastic gives you. This is the pick for day sleepers, shift workers, and anyone facing a bright summer sunrise.
  4. A weighted eye mask. Think of a weighted blanket, shrunk to mask size. The gentle, even pressure feels calming to a lot of people, the way a firm hug does, and the weight helps the mask stay put. Keep the weight modest; you want soothing pressure, not a workout for your face. Back sleepers tend to love these most.
  5. A travel mask with earplugs. The kit version: a simple mask plus earplugs, made to live in your carry-on or guest-room bag. It will not be the best mask you own, but it turns a bright, unfamiliar room, or a plane cabin, into something sleepable. Also handy at home during the weeks when the neighbours are renovating at dawn.
